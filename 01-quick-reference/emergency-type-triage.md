# Emergency Type Triage — What's Happening, What to Do First

Read this first when something has gone wrong and you don't yet know how bad it is. Find the closest match below, do the immediate actions, then go to the linked deep-dive.

## Stay-or-go decision framework (apply to any scenario)
Ask, in order:
1. **Is this location about to become more dangerous** (fire spreading, water rising, structure unstable, crowd/conflict approaching)? → Go.
2. **Do I have a safer destination I can actually reach** (route open, daylight/fuel/fitness to get there)? If no → Stay and harden your current position instead of moving into the unknown.
3. **Have authorities/reliable info issued a mandatory evacuation or a shelter-in-place order for your specific area?** Follow it unless it is clearly unsafe to comply (e.g. ordered evacuation route is now blocked/flooded).
4. **No official info at all?** Default to shelter-in-place for the first few hours while you gather information — most people who die evacuating unnecessarily die from the evacuation itself (traffic, exposure, panic), not the event.

## Natural / geological

### Earthquake
- **During**: Drop, Cover, Hold On — under sturdy furniture, away from windows/glass/heavy objects. If in bed, stay and cover head with a pillow. Outdoors: move to open ground away from buildings/power lines.
- **After**: Expect aftershocks. Check for gas leaks (smell/hissing — shut off gas main if suspected, don't use open flame/switches). Check structural damage before re-entering buildings. Check on neighbors.
- **Do not**: run outside during shaking (falling debris risk), use elevators.
- Deep dive: `07-shelter-construction/`, `02-first-aid-medical/`

### Landslide / mudslide
- **Warning signs**: cracking trees/ground, unusual sounds (rumbling, cracking), sudden increase or decrease in stream water carrying debris, doors/windows sticking (ground shifting), leaning trees/poles.
- **Immediate**: move away and *uphill/perpendicular* to the slide path, never straight downhill or straight away in the flow direction. If caught, curl into a ball to protect your head.
- **Highest risk**: after heavy rain, wildfire (destroyed root systems), or earthquake, on steep terrain.
- Deep dive: `07-shelter-construction/`

### Flood (flash flood vs. rising water)
- **Flash flood** (minutes to hours): move to high ground immediately, don't wait to gather belongings. 6 inches of fast water can knock you down; 12 inches can sweep a vehicle away — never drive or walk through moving water ("Turn Around, Don't Drown").
- **Slow rising water**: move valuables/utilities up early, shut off electricity at the breaker if water is approaching outlets (only if you can do so safely without standing in water), evacuate before roads become impassable.
- **After**: don't return until authorities clear it; floodwater may be contaminated (sewage, chemicals) — treat all contact water as unsafe, boil/purify all water per `water-purification-quicksteps.md`.
- Deep dive: `03-water/`

### Wildfire
- **Immediate**: if evacuation is ordered, leave early — don't wait to watch the fire's progress. Close windows/vents, remove flammable items from around structure if time allows.
- **Trapped**: shelter in a cleared area or vehicle (windows up, below window line, engine off, call for help), avoid canyons/dense fuel, a body of water or already-burned ground is safer than fresh fuel.
- **Air quality**: N95 mask, stay indoors with filtered air if smoke-only (not actively threatened).
- Deep dive: `07-shelter-construction/`

### Hurricane / severe storm / tornado
- **Hurricane**: evacuate per official orders (storm surge is the top killer, not wind); if sheltering, interior room, lowest level not prone to flooding, away from windows.
- **Tornado**: lowest floor, interior room/closet, away from windows, cover yourself. No basement: interior room, small closet/bathroom, avoid wide-span rooms (gyms, auditoriums).
- Deep dive: `07-shelter-construction/`

## Radiological / nuclear

**These two scenarios have opposite immediate responses — identify which one first.**

### Nuclear/radiological power plant or transport accident (no blast)
- Follow official evacuation zone guidance — this type of release is usually contained and directional (downwind), so evacuation perpendicular to wind direction, out of the affected zone, is often correct once officially indicated.
- If told to shelter in place: go inside, close windows/doors/vents, turn off HVAC intake.
- Potassium iodide (KI) tablets protect only the thyroid from radioactive iodine, and only if taken as officially directed — they are not a general radiation antidote.

### Nuclear detonation (blast seen/felt, or credible warning)
- **Immediate (first seconds)**: if you see the flash, get down and cover behind anything solid — do not look at the flash (blindness risk). Blast wave follows the flash by seconds.
- **First minutes**: get inside the nearest sturdy building, ideally below ground or in a windowless interior space, as many walls/floors between you and outside as possible.
- **Do NOT evacuate immediately** — fallout (radioactive dust) arrives within 10-20 minutes downwind and is the main lingering threat. Moving outdoors during fallout deposition is far more dangerous than staying put.
- **Shelter in place 24-48+ hours minimum** — radioactivity from fallout decays fast initially; each 7x increase in time reduces exposure roughly 10x. Staying sheltered the first 24-48 hrs matters most.
- If outside during fallout: remove outer clothing before entering shelter (removes ~90% of contamination), shower/wash exposed skin and hair with soap and water (don't scrub raw), do not use conditioner (binds particles to hair).
- Use a battery/hand-crank radio for official guidance on when it's safe to move.
- Deep dive: `07-shelter-construction/`, `03-water/` (assume outdoor water sources contaminated until confirmed otherwise)

## Public health

### Pandemic / disease outbreak
- Reduce exposure: distancing, masking (especially indoors/crowded), hand hygiene, ventilation.
- Stock a longer runway of supplies than usual (2-4+ weeks) to reduce trips out during peak transmission.
- Isolate symptomatic household members if possible; designate one caregiver to reduce household spread.
- Deep dive: `02-first-aid-medical/`

## Human conflict / social breakdown

The guidance below is about **personal safety and staying out of harm's way** — not a political position on any cause or authority. In any conflict scenario the overriding goals are: don't be a target, don't be caught in a chokepoint, protect your ability to make decisions (documents, cash, communication, family plan).

### War / armed conflict nearby
- **Indicators to watch**: military movement/checkpoints appearing, curfews announced, communications/utilities becoming unreliable, sudden price spikes or shortages, official advisories.
- **Immediate**: identify the nearest solid cover (below-ground or interior rooms away from windows, similar to tornado shelter) for shelling/airstrike risk. Have a go-bag ready (see `evacuation-bugout-checklist.md`).
- **Movement**: avoid known front lines, checkpoints of unclear allegiance, and infrastructure targets (power plants, military bases, government buildings, bridges) when possible.
- **Unexploded ordnance**: never touch or move unfamiliar military debris; note location and avoid, report if there's anyone to report to.
- Deep dive: `07-shelter-construction/`, `11-navigation-comms/`

### Civil unrest / rioting
- **Avoid crowds and choke points** (narrow streets, single exits) even if not politically engaged — you can be caught in crowd crush, tear gas, or crossfire regardless of your involvement.
- If caught in a crowd: move perpendicular to the crowd's flow toward an exit, not against it; avoid falling (crowd crush is a top cause of death, not violence itself).
- **Tear gas/pepper spray exposure**: move upwind and to fresh air immediately, flush eyes with water (not rubbing), remove contaminated clothing.
- Secure your home: away from windows during active unrest nearby, garage/vehicles secured, valuables not visible from outside.
- Keep a low profile if moving through an active area: neutral clothing, no items that could mark you as a participant on either side, do not film/engage confrontationally unless you fully accept that risk.
- Deep dive: `02-first-aid-medical/` (impact injuries, chemical irritant exposure)

### Breakdown of civil order / government or military takeover
- This is about **personal and family continuity**, not resistance or political action — the goal is to remain safe, informed, and capable of making your own decisions as conditions change.
- **Documents & identity**: keep originals *and* copies (physical + on this drive) of ID, property, medical, and financial records somewhere reachable in a fast evacuation.
- **Cash & barter**: hold some physical cash and consider barterable goods — electronic payment/banking systems can become unreliable or frozen.
- **Communications**: assume normal channels (internet, cell) may become unreliable, censored, or monitored; have a family communication plan that doesn't depend on any single channel (a predetermined meeting point is more reliable than a phone call).
- **Information discipline**: expect propaganda/misinformation from multiple sides during instability; corroborate important information across independent sources before acting on it.
- **Movement & curfews**: comply with clearly posted curfews/checkpoints where refusal creates direct physical risk to you; prioritize de-escalation in any encounter with armed personnel (hands visible, calm, comply with instructions, don't argue in the moment).
- **Community**: known, trusted neighbors are one of the most effective resources in sustained instability — coordinate supplies, information, and watch schedules where possible.
- Deep dive: `13-governance-society/`, `11-navigation-comms/`
