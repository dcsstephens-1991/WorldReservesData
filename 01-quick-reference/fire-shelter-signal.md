# Fire, Shelter & Signaling Quick Card

## Fire — priority order of need
Survival priority is usually: shelter > water > fire > food. Fire helps with warmth, water purification, signaling, and morale.

### Starting a fire
1. **Tinder** (catches spark): dry grass, bark shavings, dryer lint, cotton balls + petroleum jelly, birch bark.
2. **Kindling**: pencil-to-thumb thickness dry twigs, built in a loose teepee or log-cabin shape over the tinder.
3. **Fuel**: progressively larger dry wood added once kindling is established.
4. Ignition options ranked by reliability: lighter/matches (carry waterproofed) → ferro rod/flint & steel → friction (bow drill/hand drill, hardest, needs practice) → magnifying lens (needs direct sun).
5. Always clear a fire ring down to bare soil/rock; never leave unattended; fully extinguish (drown, stir, drown again) before leaving.

## Emergency shelter — priorities
Protect from the biggest immediate threat: wind, precipitation, ground cold (you lose heat fastest to the ground).
1. Insulate from the ground first (branches, leaves, pack) — this matters more than overhead cover in cold conditions.
2. Keep shelter just big enough for your body — smaller space = less heat to retain.
3. Types:
   - **Debris hut**: ridge pole + leaning sticks + thick pile of leaves/debris (12+ inches) over frame. Good in forested temperate areas.
   - **Lean-to**: single angled wall against wind direction, tarp or bark, best paired with a fire reflecting off the open side.
   - **Snow shelter (quinzhee/snow cave)**: dig into a drift, keep walls 12+ inches thick, ventilation hole essential (CO2/suffocation risk).
4. Site selection: avoid dead trees/branches overhead, flash-flood-prone low ground, ridgelines exposed to wind/lightning, insect nests.

## Signaling for rescue
- **Fire/smoke**: three fires in a triangle or line is a recognized distress signal. Green boughs on a hot fire make thick white smoke (daytime); the fire itself is visible at night.
- **Ground-to-air symbols**: large (10+ ft), high-contrast materials or trenches. "X" = need medical assistance, "V" = need assistance, arrow = direction of travel.
- **Mirror/reflective signal**: aim reflected sunlight by sighting through a hole/notch in the mirror at the target; sweep across the horizon if no target is visible yet.
- **Whistle/sound**: three blasts/shouts/flashes = internationally recognized distress signal (pause, repeat).
- **Stay put** if a search is likely and you're already found/reported missing — moving makes you harder to find. Move only if you must for water/safety, and leave clear directional markers.
