# Sanitation Infrastructure — Construction Basics

Construction-focused companion to `02-first-aid-medical/sanitation-hygiene.md`, which covers the health rationale and usage practices.

## Pit latrine construction
- Dig at least 30m/100ft from any water source, downhill/downstream of it, and away from the foundation of any structure (undermining risk to nearby buildings if dug too close).
- Depth: 1.5-3m/5-10ft depending on expected use duration and household size — deeper for longer-term or higher-use installations.
- Lining the pit (with brick, stone, or other durable material) prevents collapse, especially in loose/sandy soil — an unlined pit in unstable soil is a real safety hazard, not just a durability issue.
- A simple platform/slab over the pit with a properly sized opening, plus a fitted cover/lid, reduces odor, fly access, and accident risk.
- A vent pipe from the pit to above roof height, screened at the top, meaningfully reduces both odor and flies inside an enclosed latrine structure (this is the core idea behind a "VIP" — ventilated improved pit — latrine, a widely used low-cost design).

## Composting toilet basics
- Separates solid waste for aerobic decomposition (with a carbon-rich cover material like sawdust or ash added after each use) rather than pit disposal — produces usable compost over time and avoids groundwater contamination risk entirely, useful where a dug pit isn't practical (high water table, rocky ground).
- Requires more active management (turning/monitoring the compost, ensuring adequate carbon material) than a simple pit latrine.

## Greywater management
- Greywater (from washing, not toilet waste) can often be directed to a simple gravel-filled soak pit or used for non-food-crop irrigation rather than being wasted or allowed to pool.
- Avoid greywater containing harsh chemicals (strong soaps/detergents in quantity) if it will be used for irrigation.

## General siting principle
Any sanitation infrastructure should be positioned with prevailing wind direction in mind relative to living/cooking areas, in addition to the water-source distance rule above.
