# Basic Carpentry & Joinery

Starter overview for building with wood without power tools or manufactured hardware.

## Core joinery concepts (no metal fasteners required)
- **Notched joints** (e.g. log cabin corner notching) — logs/timbers cut with matching notches that interlock under their own weight, a foundational technique for log structures.
- **Mortise and tenon** — a projecting tenon on one piece fits into a cut mortise (slot) on another; one of the strongest traditional wood joints, used for frames.
- **Lashing/binding** — rope, vine, or cordage wrapped and tightened around a joint (e.g. lashing poles into an A-frame); fast, requires no cutting precision, weaker than interlocking joints but very practical for quick construction.
- **Pegged joints** — a hole drilled through an assembled joint and a wooden peg driven through to lock it, used historically before widespread metal fastener availability (timber framing tradition).

## Basic tool set (minimum viable)
- Axe (felling and general shaping)
- Hand saw
- Chisel and mallet
- Hand drill/auger (for peg holes)
- Measuring/marking tools (string line, straightedge)
- A drawknife is a significant efficiency upgrade for shaping round stock but not strictly required to start

## Wood selection basics
- Green (freshly cut) wood is easier to work but shrinks/warps as it dries — account for this in joinery (a peg or lashing can be re-tightened; a tight-fit interlocking joint made with green wood may loosen as it dries).
- Straight-grained wood is stronger and easier to work than twisted-grain wood.
- Rot resistance varies significantly by species — some woods (varies regionally) are far better suited to ground contact or wet exposure; a load-bearing outdoor structure benefits from knowing locally rot-resistant species.
