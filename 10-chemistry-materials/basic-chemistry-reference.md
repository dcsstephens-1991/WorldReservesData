# Basic Chemistry Reference

Foundational concepts referenced throughout the archive (soap-making, fuels, materials, medicine preparation). Not a full chemistry course — see `12-science-education/` for that once built out.

## Core concepts
- **Elements, compounds, mixtures**: elements are pure substances that can't be broken down further chemically; compounds are two or more elements chemically bonded (fixed ratio, new properties); mixtures are physically combined substances that retain their individual properties and can often be separated by physical means (filtering, evaporation, etc.).
- **Acids and bases (pH)**: pH measures how acidic or basic (alkaline) a solution is, on a roughly 0-14 scale, 7 being neutral. Acids (low pH) and bases (high pH) react with and can neutralize each other. Practical relevance: soap-making (`soap-and-cleaning-agents.md`) is fundamentally an acid-base-type reaction (saponification); many food preservation and cleaning applications depend on pH.
- **Oxidation and combustion**: burning is a rapid oxidation reaction (combining with oxygen, releasing energy as heat/light) — relevant to every fuel discussion in `08-energy-power/`. Slower oxidation (like rusting metal) is the same basic chemistry at a much slower rate.
- **Solutions and concentration**: how much of a substance is dissolved in another (e.g. salt in water) — relevant to water purification dosing (`01-quick-reference/water-purification-quicksteps.md`), medicine dosing caution (`05-plants-fungi/medicinal-plants-general-principles.md`), and food preservation (brining, pickling).

## Basic lab safety principles (apply to any hands-on chemistry, from soap-making to biodiesel)
- Always add acid to water, not water to acid, when diluting concentrated acids — adding water to concentrated acid can cause a violent, splattering exothermic reaction; the reverse order is safer.
- Work in ventilated spaces — many chemical reactions produce fumes that are harmful in enclosed spaces.
- Never mix chemicals without knowing what the reaction produces — some common household chemical combinations (e.g. bleach and ammonia) produce dangerous toxic gas.
- Label containers clearly and never store chemicals in unlabeled or food-labeled containers — a major cause of accidental poisoning.
- Keep a source of clean water nearby for rinsing skin/eyes in case of contact with a caustic or irritant substance.
