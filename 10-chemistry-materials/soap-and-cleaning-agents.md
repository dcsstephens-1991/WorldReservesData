# Soap & Cleaning Agents

## Basic soap-making chemistry (saponification)
- Soap is made by reacting a fat or oil with an alkali (traditionally lye — sodium hydroxide for hard bar soap, or potassium hydroxide for softer/liquid soap) — this reaction is called saponification.
- **Lye is caustic and genuinely dangerous to handle carelessly** — it causes serious chemical burns on contact with skin/eyes. Always wear eye protection and gloves, work in a ventilated area, and follow the acid/water safety principle from `basic-chemistry-reference.md` (add lye to water, not water to lye, when dissolving it — same splatter-risk logic).
- Traditional lye source: wood ash leached with water (producing a lye solution, historically how soap was made before manufactured lye was available) — a real, functional method, though the resulting lye's concentration is harder to control precisely than manufactured lye, making the resulting soap's strength less predictable.
- Basic process: dissolve lye in water (careful, exothermic — the solution heats up), combine with melted fat/oil at a compatible temperature, mix until the mixture thickens and reaches "trace" (a visible thickening indicating the saponification reaction is progressing), pour into molds, and let cure — the reaction continues after pouring, and a curing period (commonly cited as around 4-6 weeks for bar soap) allows excess moisture to evaporate and the soap to fully saponify and mellow, producing a milder final bar.
- Fat/oil source matters for the final soap's properties — different fats produce different hardness, lather, and skin-feel; animal fat (tallow, lard) and various plant oils are all traditional soap-making inputs.

## Basic cleaning agents beyond soap
- **Ash and sand** — usable as mild abrasive cleaners for dishes/hands when soap isn't available, referenced in `02-first-aid-medical/sanitation-hygiene.md` as a handwashing substitute.
- **Vinegar (dilute acetic acid)** — a mild acid useful for general cleaning and some disinfecting applications, widely available if fermentation (see `04-food-agriculture/` preservation content) is set up.
- **Boiling water** — itself a basic disinfecting method for surfaces/utensils when chemical cleaners aren't available.
