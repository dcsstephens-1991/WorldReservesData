# Fuels & Combustion — Chemistry Basics

Chemistry-focused companion to `08-energy-power/fuels-basics.md`, which covers the practical/production side.

## What combustion actually is
Combustion is a rapid oxidation reaction: a fuel (typically carbon and hydrogen-rich material — wood, charcoal, oil, biogas) reacts with oxygen, releasing energy as heat and light, and producing byproducts (primarily carbon dioxide and water vapor with complete combustion; carbon monoxide, soot/particulates, and other compounds with incomplete combustion).

## Complete vs. incomplete combustion
- **Complete combustion** requires adequate oxygen supply relative to the fuel being burned — produces more heat per unit of fuel and fewer harmful byproducts.
- **Incomplete combustion** (insufficient oxygen — a common result of a poorly ventilated fire or stove) produces carbon monoxide, a colorless, odorless, genuinely lethal gas — this is the chemistry behind why enclosed-space ventilation is repeatedly emphasized throughout this archive (`07-shelter-construction/emergency-shelter-basics.md`, `08-energy-power/fuels-basics.md`) whenever combustion happens indoors.
- Practical implication: a well-designed fire/stove with good airflow burns more efficiently (more heat, less fuel used, less smoke) than a smothered, poorly ventilated one.

## Why different fuels burn differently
- Energy content (heat released per unit of fuel) varies significantly by fuel type — this is why charcoal (more energy-dense after processing) burns hotter than the raw wood it was made from.
- Moisture content matters enormously — wet/green wood wastes a significant portion of its combustion energy just evaporating the water it contains, which is the chemistry reason seasoned (dried) wood burns more efficiently, as noted in `08-energy-power/fuels-basics.md`.

## Relevance to fire safety
Understanding that fire requires fuel, oxygen, and heat together (the "fire triangle") explains why fire suppression strategies work by removing one of the three: water cools (removes heat), smothering removes oxygen, and clearing a firebreak removes fuel — directly relevant to the wildfire response in `01-quick-reference/emergency-type-triage.md`.
