# Foundational Concepts Cheat Sheet

Quick-reference formulas and concepts likely to be needed across the archive's practical folders, until full textbook content is added.

## Basic math
- **Area**: rectangle = length × width; triangle = ½ × base × height; circle = π × radius².
- **Volume**: rectangular box = length × width × height; cylinder = π × radius² × height.
- **Percentages**: part ÷ whole × 100 = percentage. Useful for concentration/dosing calculations (e.g. water purification dosing, soap-making ratios).
- **Unit conversion basics**: keep a units-and-conversions reference on hand (metric ⟷ imperial) since sourcing and equipment may use either system.

## Basic physics
- **Force = mass × acceleration** (Newton's second law) — the foundation of understanding load, structural stress, and simple machines.
- **Simple machines** (lever, pulley, inclined plane, wheel and axle, wedge, screw) all trade force for distance (or vice versa) — a longer lever arm or more pulleys means less force needed to move a load, at the cost of moving it a shorter distance per unit of effort, or requiring more total motion. Directly relevant to `07-shelter-construction/`, `09-tools-manufacturing/`, and `08-energy-power/mechanical-power-basics.md`.
- **Energy is never created or destroyed, only converted** (conservation of energy) — relevant to understanding why every energy conversion step (mechanical → electrical → chemical storage → mechanical again, for example) loses some usable energy as heat, which is why direct mechanical solutions are often more efficient than converting through electricity, as noted in `08-energy-power/mechanical-power-basics.md`.

## Basic chemistry (see `10-chemistry-materials/basic-chemistry-reference.md` for more)
- pH scale: 0-14, 7 neutral, lower = more acidic, higher = more basic (alkaline).
- Combustion = fuel + oxygen → heat/light + byproducts.

## Basic biology
- **Cells** are the basic unit of life; all living things are made of one or more cells.
- **Ecosystems** function through energy flow (generally starting with plants converting sunlight to usable energy via photosynthesis) and nutrient cycling (decomposition returning nutrients to the soil) — relevant to understanding sustainable agriculture and why soil health matters (`04-food-agriculture/`).
- **Germ theory**: infectious disease is caused by microorganisms (bacteria, viruses, parasites, fungi) transmitted between hosts — the entire basis for the sanitation and hygiene practices in `02-first-aid-medical/sanitation-hygiene.md`.

## Scientific method (worth internalizing as a practical tool, not just an academic concept)
1. Observe something and ask a question.
2. Form a hypothesis (a testable explanation).
3. Test it, ideally changing only one variable at a time so you know what actually caused any observed result.
4. Draw a conclusion based on the actual result, and revise the hypothesis if the evidence doesn't support it.
This process is directly applicable to practical troubleshooting throughout this archive — see `09-tools-manufacturing/general-repair-principles.md` for an applied version of the same logic.
