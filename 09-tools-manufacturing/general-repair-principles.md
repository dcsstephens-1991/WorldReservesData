# General Repair Principles

A repair mindset — applicable across mechanical, electrical, and general equipment — rather than device-specific instructions (which age quickly and vary too much to generalize).

## Diagnostic approach
1. **Confirm the actual symptom before assuming a cause** — don't skip straight to a fix based on a guess; observe what's actually happening first.
2. **Check the simple/common causes before the complex/rare ones** — a dead battery is far more likely than a failed circuit board; a loose connection is far more likely than a component failure.
3. **Isolate the problem** — test/inspect one component or connection point at a time rather than replacing multiple things at once, so you actually learn what was wrong (important when parts are scarce and you can't afford to guess wrong repeatedly).
4. **Compare to a known-working reference** when possible — a working example of the same item, or documentation, tells you what "normal" looks like.

## General mechanical repair
- Most mechanical failures trace back to one of a few common categories: wear (parts degrading with use), lubrication failure (friction/heat damage from inadequate lubrication), fastener failure (loose/broken/corroded fasteners), or contamination (dirt/debris interfering with moving parts).
- Cleaning and proper lubrication resolve a surprising fraction of "broken" mechanical equipment — check these before assuming a part needs replacement.
- Keep failed/worn parts as patterns for fabricating replacements, and as a source of raw material — see `09-tools-manufacturing/` blacksmithing and woodworking files for fabrication.

## General electrical repair
- Most electrical failures trace back to: a blown fuse/breaker (protective device doing its job — find and fix the underlying overcurrent cause before just replacing the fuse), a loose/corroded connection, or a failed component.
- A multimeter (measuring voltage, resistance/continuity, and current) is the single most valuable diagnostic tool for electrical work — learning to use one is a high-value skill.
- Always treat a circuit as live until confirmed otherwise — see `08-energy-power/off-grid-electrical-basics.md` for wiring safety principles.

## Fastener and adhesive basics
- Learn to identify and improvise common fastener types (threads, pins, wedges) since manufactured replacement parts won't always be available — a well-fitted wooden peg or forged pin can substitute for a manufactured bolt in many applications.
- Natural adhesives (hide glue from animal collagen, pine pitch/resin-based glues) are traditional, functional options when manufactured adhesives aren't available — worth knowing as a category even before detailed recipes are added.

## Preventive maintenance mindset
Regular inspection and minor maintenance (cleaning, lubrication, tightening) prevents most major failures — in a context where replacement parts/tools are scarce, prevention is disproportionately more valuable than in normal times where replacement is easy.
