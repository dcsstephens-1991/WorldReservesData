# Temperate East Asia — Health Considerations

## Climate-driven risks
- Four distinct seasons across most of this subregion; monsoon-influenced summer rains; typhoon exposure along the eastern coast (Japan, Taiwan, coastal China, Korea) roughly June–October.
- Earthquake and tsunami risk is significant along the Japanese archipelago and parts of the Chinese/Taiwanese coast.

## Disease patterns
- **Japanese encephalitis** — present in rural rice-farming areas; vaccination where reachable.
- Seasonal influenza patterns are significant given high population density in parts of this subregion.
- Strong healthcare infrastructure in urban Japan/Korea generally reduces overall risk relative to less-resourced subregions in this archive.

## Dangerous species
See `06-animals-insects/` for identification detail:
- Mamushi pit viper (Japan, Korea, parts of China) — the most medically significant snake across this subregion.
- Asian giant hornet present in parts of this region — very painful, occasionally dangerous stings, especially with multiple stings or in an allergic individual.
