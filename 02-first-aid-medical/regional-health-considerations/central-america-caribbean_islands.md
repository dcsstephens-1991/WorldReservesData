# Caribbean Islands — Health Considerations

## Climate-driven risks
- Hurricane season roughly June–November, peaking August–October — the dominant environmental risk across this subregion.
- Many islands have limited internal evacuation routes/high ground — factor this heavily into stay-or-go decisions ahead of a storm.

## Disease patterns
- **Dengue, chikungunya, Zika** — widespread, same *Aedes* mosquito vector as the Central American mainland.
- **Cholera** — has persisted in parts of Haiti following the 2010 outbreak; reinforces water/sanitation discipline in affected areas.
- **Leptospirosis** — risk increases after flooding from heavy rain/hurricanes.

## Dangerous species
See `06-animals-insects/` for identification detail:
- Most islands are comparatively snake-poor relative to the Central American mainland — land-animal bite risk is lower here than most subregions in this archive.
- **Lionfish** — invasive, venomous spines, now widespread across Caribbean reefs; avoid handling.
- Fire coral and various jellyfish in coastal waters; some islands have scorpions.

## Notes
- Given the hurricane exposure and constrained evacuation routes on smaller islands, treat storm-season preparation (see `01-quick-reference/evacuation-bugout-checklist.md`) as a standing priority here, not a reactive one.
