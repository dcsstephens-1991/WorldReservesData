# Antarctic — Health Considerations

This is a fundamentally different risk profile from every other file in this set — there is essentially no self-sufficient civilian survival scenario here outside an organized, supported research program.

## Climate-driven risks
- The coldest, driest, windiest continent on Earth — extreme cold is a constant, not a seasonal event.
- Near-total isolation from outside medical support for much of the year, especially during the austral winter when most stations are unreachable by air or sea.

## Disease patterns
- Minimal infectious disease burden, due to extreme isolation and a small, health-screened population.
- Most medical risk here is environmental and traumatic (cold injury, falls, crevasse hazards) rather than infectious.

## Dangerous species
See `06-animals-insects/` for identification detail:
- No native land predators or venomous species.
- Coastal/open-water hazards involve marine wildlife (leopard seals near ice edges) rather than the bite/sting risks seen elsewhere in this archive.

## Notes
- Medical evacuation can be entirely unavailable for months during the winter season — prevention and redundancy in any expedition planning matter more here than anywhere else in this archive. This file is informational context, not a substitute for the specialized training and support any real Antarctic expedition requires.
