# Congo Basin — Health Considerations

## Climate-driven risks
- Dense rainforest, consistently high heat and humidity year-round with limited seasonal variation compared to the wider region.
- Heavy rainfall complicates travel — roads become impassable, extending evacuation times significantly.

## Disease patterns
- **Ebola and Marburg virus disease** — this subregion has historically been the site of episodic outbreaks; not a constant background risk, but check local/WHO outbreak advisories before travel and avoid contact with sick or dead wildlife (especially primates and bats) at all times as a standing precaution.
- **Mpox (monkeypox)** — endemic in parts of this subregion; avoid contact with rodents/primates and their body fluids, cook bushmeat thoroughly.
- **Malaria** — among the highest transmission intensity in the world in parts of this subregion; bed nets and prompt treatment are essential, not optional.
- **African trypanosomiasis (sleeping sickness)** — tsetse fly-borne, present in defined belts through the basin; avoid known tsetse habitat (riverine woodland/brush) where possible, cover skin.
- **Schistosomiasis** — from freshwater snails in slow-moving rivers/lakes; avoid wading/swimming in untreated fresh water.

## Dangerous species
See `06-animals-insects/` for identification detail. Named here for cross-reference:
- Gaboon viper, forest cobra
- Forest elephants — unpredictable, capable of serious injury; give wide berth
- Driver/safari ants — swarms can overwhelm; move away immediately if encountered, don't stand still

## Notes
- This is the least road-accessible subregion in this file set — plan for multi-day evacuation timelines to any advanced medical facility, and weight prevention (bed nets, water treatment, avoiding wildlife contact) more heavily than treatment-after-the-fact accordingly.
