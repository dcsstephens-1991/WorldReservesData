# Indo-Gangetic Plains — Health Considerations

## Climate-driven risks
- Among the most severe heat-wave regions in the world in the pre-monsoon period (roughly April–June) — dangerously high heat combined with humidity in some areas; heat illness prevention should be a default daily precaution in this season, not a reaction.
- Monsoon flooding (roughly June–September) across the river plains — flash and slow-rise flooding both occur depending on location.
- Dense agricultural land with heavy irrigation — standing water is widespread, elevating mosquito-borne disease risk.

## Disease patterns
- **Dengue and chikungunya** — widespread, day-biting mosquito vectors; bite prevention (repellent, covering skin, eliminating standing water near dwellings) is the primary defense.
- **Malaria** — present, generally lower intensity than in Sub-Saharan Africa but still significant in parts of this subregion.
- **Japanese encephalitis** — associated with rice-paddy/pig-farming areas; vaccination where reachable, bite prevention otherwise.
- **Typhoid and cholera** — sharp seasonal spikes around monsoon flooding; water treatment discipline matters most in this window.
- **Heat-related illness** — treat as a leading cause of preventable death in the pre-monsoon season here, on par with infectious disease risk.

## Dangerous species
See `06-animals-insects/` for identification detail. The "Big Four" venomous snakes responsible for the large majority of snakebite deaths in this subregion:
- Indian cobra
- Russell's viper
- Common krait (nocturnal, bites often occur while people sleep on the ground — sleeping off the ground or under a tucked-in net reduces risk)
- Saw-scaled viper

## Notes
- Snakebite antivenom access varies significantly by how rural the specific location is — identifying the nearest source before it's needed is worth the effort here.
