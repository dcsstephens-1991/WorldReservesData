# Australian Arid Interior & Temperate South — Health Considerations

## Climate-driven risks
- Arid interior brings extreme heat and very low water availability; temperate south has a normal four-season climate.
- Extreme remoteness in the interior means very long evacuation times — water and communication planning are paramount there.

## Disease patterns
- Relatively low infectious disease burden by global standards; lower humidity limits some mosquito-borne disease relative to the tropical north.

## Dangerous species
See `06-animals-insects/` for identification detail:
- Australia has an unusually high concentration of the world's most venomous snakes (e.g. inland taipan, eastern brown snake) and venomous spiders (funnel-web, redback) — bite/sting risk here is disproportionate to disease risk, the opposite pattern from most tropical subregions in this archive.

## Notes
- Given the Outback's remoteness, treat this as one of the highest evacuation-time subregions in the archive despite the comparatively low disease burden.
