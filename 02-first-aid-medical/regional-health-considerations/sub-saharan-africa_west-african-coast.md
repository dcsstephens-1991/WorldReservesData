# West African Coast — Health Considerations

## Climate-driven risks
- High humidity and heat year-round; monsoon rains roughly June–October bring flooding risk in low-lying coastal and river-delta areas.
- Coastal erosion/storm surge risk during heavy rain season.

## Disease patterns
- **Lassa fever** — rodent-borne (contact with infected rat urine/droppings, contaminated food/surfaces), endemic in this subregion especially Nigeria, Sierra Leone, Liberia, Guinea; keep food covered and sealed against rodents, avoid handling/eating rodents caught for food without thorough cooking.
- **Malaria** — high, year-round transmission across the subregion; bed nets and prompt fever treatment are high-value.
- **Yellow fever** — endemic; vaccination (where reachable) is the primary defense, mosquito bite prevention otherwise.
- **Onchocerciasis (river blindness)** — transmitted by blackflies near fast-flowing rivers; avoid prolonged exposure near breeding sites where known to be present.
- **Cholera** — periodic outbreaks, especially in dense urban/informal settlements with limited sanitation — reinforces water/sanitation discipline.

## Dangerous species
See `06-animals-insects/` for identification detail. Named here for cross-reference:
- Gaboon viper (large, well-camouflaged, potent venom)
- Black-necked spitting cobra (can spray venom toward the eyes — avoid the face, flush immediately if sprayed)
- West African crocodile in rivers/estuaries

## Notes
- Rural areas often have limited road access to advanced medical care — factor longer evacuation timelines into any severe-injury response plan here.
