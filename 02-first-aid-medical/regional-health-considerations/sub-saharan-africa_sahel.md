# Sahel — Health Considerations

## Climate-driven risks
- Semi-arid to arid — extreme heat, especially in the dry season; heat illness risk is a constant baseline concern, not a seasonal spike.
- Harmattan wind (roughly November–March) carries fine dust from the Sahara — respiratory irritation, reduced visibility.
- Water scarcity is a dominant, year-round survival factor here more than almost any other subregion in this set — planning around water access should take priority over most other preparation.

## Disease patterns
- **Meningococcal meningitis** — this subregion is the classic "meningitis belt," with seasonal epidemics concentrated in the hot, dry season (roughly December–June); stiff neck, high fever, and severe headache warrant urgent attention, vaccination where reachable is the strongest defense.
- **Malnutrition-related vulnerability** — recurring drought conditions increase vulnerability to infectious disease generally; food security planning is itself a health intervention in this subregion.
- **Cholera** — periodic outbreaks in some areas, tied to water access disruption.

## Dangerous species
See `06-animals-insects/` for identification detail. Named here for cross-reference:
- Saw-scaled viper — small but responsible for a disproportionate share of snakebite deaths regionally due to its aggressive defensive behavior and potent venom; watch step placement especially at dawn/dusk
- Scorpions — several medically significant species present

## Notes
- Given the water-scarcity baseline, treat every water-purification method in `03-water/` as core survival infrastructure here, not a backup plan.
