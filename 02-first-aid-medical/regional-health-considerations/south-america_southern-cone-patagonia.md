# Southern Cone & Patagonia — Health Considerations

## Climate-driven risks
- Temperate to subpolar climate in the far south; Patagonia is known for sustained, strong winds that complicate fire-building, shelter, and general exposure management beyond what temperature alone suggests.
- Cold-weather exposure risk increases significantly moving south toward Tierra del Fuego.

## Disease patterns
- Generally lower tropical infectious disease burden than the rest of South America in this file set.
- **Hantavirus** — documented in some rural areas with rodent exposure (agricultural work, opening closed rural buildings); ventilate before entering and avoid dry-sweeping droppings, same precaution as the North America temperate file.

## Dangerous species
See `06-animals-insects/` for identification detail:
- Far fewer highly venomous species than tropical South America; some pit viper species present in the northern portion of this subregion (e.g. yarará species).

## Notes
- Sustained wind is a distinct, often underestimated hazard here — factor wind chill and wind-driven fire/shelter difficulty into planning separately from ambient temperature.
