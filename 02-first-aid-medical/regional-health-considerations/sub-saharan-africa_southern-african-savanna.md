# Southern African Savanna — Health Considerations

## Climate-driven risks
- Strong dry-season/wet-season split; dry season (roughly May–October in most of the subregion) brings elevated wildfire risk across savanna grassland.
- Arid western portion (Kalahari, Namib) has large diurnal temperature swings — hot days, cold nights — plan layered clothing and water reserves accordingly.
- Temperate southern coastal areas (South Africa's Cape region) are markedly milder than the savanna interior — don't apply interior heat/malaria assumptions to the whole subregion.

## Disease patterns
- **Malaria** — present in lower-lying and northern parts (Zambia, Zimbabwe, Mozambique, northern South Africa/Kruger area); much lower to absent risk in South Africa's temperate south and highveld — check specific location.
- **African tick bite fever** (rickettsial, tick-borne) — common in game/bush areas; fever, headache, and a characteristic dark scab (eschar) at the bite site.
- **Schistosomiasis (bilharzia)** — present in many dams, rivers, and irrigation canals; avoid swimming/wading in untreated fresh water where local advisories flag it.

## Dangerous species
See `06-animals-insects/` for identification detail. Named here for cross-reference:
- Black mamba, puff adder, boomslang
- Hippos — statistically among the most dangerous animals to humans in the region; never approach or camp close to hippo water access routes
- Crocodiles near rivers/dams
- Lions, leopards, elephants, and Cape buffalo in and around wildlife areas — maintain safe distance and follow local guidance in any game-adjacent zone

## Notes
- Rural/bush medical evacuation can be slow — many game and farming areas are far from hospitals; a satellite communication plan is disproportionately valuable here relative to denser regions.
