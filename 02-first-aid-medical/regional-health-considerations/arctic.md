# Arctic — Health Considerations

## Climate-driven risks
- Extreme cold, especially in winter; permafrost terrain; extreme seasonal daylight variation (near-continuous daylight in summer, near-continuous darkness in winter) affects sleep, activity planning, and morale.
- Hypothermia and frostbite risk is dominant and near-constant — not a seasonal spike but a standing baseline concern.

## Disease patterns
- Low infectious disease burden overall, due to sparse population and cold-limited vectors.
- **Trichinosis** — risk from undercooked wild game (bears, walrus, some marine mammals) in traditional/subsistence hunting contexts; cook thoroughly.

## Dangerous species
See `06-animals-insects/` for identification detail:
- **Polar bears** — a serious, potentially lethal predation risk across much of this region, unlike almost anywhere else in this archive. Treat bear-avoidance and camp discipline (food storage away from sleeping areas, noise/visual deterrents, situational awareness) as a primary safety item, not an afterthought.

## Notes
- Evacuation timelines can be extremely long and weather-dependent — err heavily toward prevention and conservative decision-making rather than reactive treatment.
