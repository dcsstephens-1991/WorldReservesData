# Temperate North America — Health Considerations

## Climate-driven risks
- Wide seasonal swing: heat illness a serious summer risk in southern/interior areas (esp. Desert Southwest subregion), hypothermia/frostbite a serious winter risk across the north and at elevation.
- Tick season (spring–fall) brings vector-borne disease risk (below) — check for ticks after time in brush/tall grass, remove promptly with fine-tipped tweezers close to the skin.
- Tornado-related trauma risk in the central "Tornado Alley" band; hurricane-related risk along the Gulf and Atlantic coasts (seasonal, roughly June–November).

## Disease patterns
- **Lyme disease** (tick-borne) — most concentrated in the Northeast, Mid-Atlantic, and Upper Midwest; early sign is often a expanding "bullseye" rash at the bite site, but not always present — fever/fatigue/joint pain after a tick bite warrants attention.
- **Rocky Mountain spotted fever** (tick-borne) — despite the name, more cases occur in the south-central US than the Rockies; fever, headache, rash.
- **West Nile virus** (mosquito-borne) — widespread, most cases mild but can cause serious neurological illness in a minority.
- **Hantavirus** (rodent droppings/urine, inhaled) — risk when cleaning enclosed spaces with rodent infestation (cabins, sheds); ventilate and avoid dry-sweeping droppings.
- **Waterborne giardia/cryptosporidium** — present in surface water even in pristine-looking wilderness streams; always treat.

## Dangerous species
See `06-animals-insects/` for identification detail. Named here for cross-reference:
- Venomous snakes: rattlesnakes (widespread), copperheads and cottonmouths (southeast/south-central)
- Black widow and brown recluse spiders (widespread, more common in southern half)
- Black bears (widespread) and grizzly/brown bears (Pacific Northwest, northern Rockies, western Canada) — food storage discipline matters in bear country

## Notes
- Elevation-related risk (altitude sickness) relevant in Rocky Mountain, Cascade, and Sierra Nevada subregions above ~2,500m/8,000ft — ascend gradually, watch for headache/nausea/dizziness that doesn't improve with rest.
- Regionally variable water access — arid Southwest requires more deliberate water planning than the humid Southeast/Northeast.
