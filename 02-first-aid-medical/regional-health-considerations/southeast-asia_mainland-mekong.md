# Mainland Southeast Asia (Mekong) — Health Considerations

## Climate-driven risks
- Monsoon flooding tied to the Mekong River's seasonal flood pulse — predictable annually but locally severe; low-lying delta areas (southern Vietnam, Cambodia's Tonlé Sap basin) see the most extensive flooding.
- High humidity heat stress, especially in the pre-monsoon hot season (roughly March–May).

## Disease patterns
- **Dengue** — widespread across the subregion, a leading cause of illness; bite prevention primary defense.
- **Malaria** — present especially in forested and border/highland areas, lower risk in major lowland cities.
- **Japanese encephalitis** — associated with rice-paddy/pig-farming areas, vaccination where reachable.
- **Melioidosis** — a soil- and water-dwelling bacterium notably significant in this subregion (especially northeastern Thailand); infection risk rises with skin contact with soil/water during rainy season, particularly with open wounds or in people with diabetes/chronic conditions — avoid unnecessary contact with mud/standing water when injured.

## Dangerous species
See `06-animals-insects/` for identification detail:
- Monocled cobra, king cobra (the world's longest venomous snake, present in forested areas of this subregion)
- Malayan pit viper
- Various krait species (nocturnal, similar precautions as South Asia's common krait)

## Notes
- Rural/forested border areas can have significantly slower medical evacuation than the subregion's major cities — factor this into risk decisions when traveling away from urban centers.
