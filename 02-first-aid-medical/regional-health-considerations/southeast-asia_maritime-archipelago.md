# Maritime/Archipelago Southeast Asia — Health Considerations

## Climate-driven risks
- Typhoon/cyclone exposure, especially the Philippines — track official storm advisories, this subregion sees some of the most frequent tropical cyclone activity in the world.
- Sits on the Pacific "Ring of Fire" — active volcanism (ashfall respiratory hazard, evacuation planning near active volcanoes) and high earthquake/tsunami risk along subduction zones.
- High year-round heat and humidity, minimal seasonal temperature variation near the equator.

## Disease patterns
- **Dengue** — very widespread, a leading cause of illness across the subregion.
- **Malaria** — significant on some islands (notably parts of Indonesian Papua), much lower in dense urban centers (e.g. Jakarta, Manila metro core) — check specific location/island.
- **Typhoid** — reinforces general water/food safety discipline.
- **Leptospirosis** — recurring risk after flooding, common in this subregion's frequent heavy-rain events.
- **Lymphatic filariasis** — present in some areas; mosquito bite prevention relevant.

## Dangerous species
See `06-animals-insects/` for identification detail:
- Saltwater crocodile — especially significant human-safety concern in parts of this subregion (Indonesia, parts of the Philippines); avoid swimming/wading in known crocodile habitat, including some river mouths and mangrove areas.
- King cobra, various pit vipers
- Box jellyfish and other venomous marine life along some coasts — check local advisories before swimming, especially in northern Australia-adjacent waters and parts of Indonesia
- Reticulated python — not venomous but among the largest snakes in the world; capable of serious injury to a human in rare cases

## Notes
- Volcanic and seismic hazard planning (ashfall masks, evacuation routes, tsunami awareness for coastal areas) deserves specific attention in this subregion beyond the general disaster guidance in `01-quick-reference/emergency-type-triage.md`.
