# South Asian Peninsula & Sri Lanka — Health Considerations

## Climate-driven risks
- Cyclone exposure along both coasts (Bay of Bengal side and Arabian Sea side), seasonal — track official cyclone advisories in coastal areas.
- Monsoon flooding, though generally less extreme than the Indo-Gangetic Plains further north.
- High year-round heat/humidity in coastal zones; the interior Deccan Plateau is drier with a more pronounced hot season.

## Disease patterns
- **Dengue and chikungunya** — widespread across the subregion, day-biting mosquito vectors, bite prevention is primary defense.
- **Malaria** — present in parts of the subregion, generally patchier than further north.
- **Leptospirosis** — notable risk after monsoon flooding, especially in areas with agricultural/livestock water contact; avoid wading through floodwater with open cuts.
- **Lymphatic filariasis** — present in some coastal areas; mosquito bite prevention is the relevant defense.

## Dangerous species
See `06-animals-insects/` for identification detail:
- Russell's viper, saw-scaled viper, Indian cobra (all present on the peninsula; Sri Lanka has its own overlapping but distinct snake fauna, including additional endemic species)
- Sea snakes along both coastlines — generally non-aggressive but venomous; avoid handling

## Notes
- Coastal population density here is high in many areas, which generally means faster access to medical care than the more remote subregions in this set — but cyclone events can disrupt that access sharply and suddenly.
