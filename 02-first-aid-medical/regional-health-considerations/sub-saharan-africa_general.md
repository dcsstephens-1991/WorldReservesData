# Sub-Saharan Africa — General Health Considerations

## Climate-driven risks
- Heat illness risk year-round in lower-elevation/savanna zones; highland zones (East African Highlands, parts of Southern Africa) more temperate.
- Rainy-season flooding increases waterborne disease and vector-breeding risk in many subregions.

## Disease patterns
- **Malaria** — present across most of the region (varies by elevation and season), a leading cause of preventable death; mosquito netting (ideally insecticide-treated), covering skin at dusk/dawn, and prompt treatment of fever are all high-value.
- **Typhoid and other water/foodborne illness** — reinforces water purification and sanitation discipline (`03-water/`, `sanitation-hygiene.md`) as top priority.
- **Schistosomiasis** — parasite from freshwater snails in slow-moving/stagnant fresh water in many subregions; avoid wading/swimming in untreated freshwater where this is known to be present.
- **Ebola/Marburg and other viral hemorrhagic fevers** — historically episodic outbreaks in specific areas (Central and parts of East Africa); not a constant background risk but warrants attention to local outbreak advisories.
- **Sleeping sickness (trypanosomiasis)** — tsetse fly-borne, present in defined belts across the region; avoid known tsetse habitat (woodland/brush near water) where possible.

## Dangerous species
See `06-animals-insects/` for identification detail. Named here for cross-reference:
- Venomous snakes: black mamba, puff adder, various cobra species (widespread but distribution varies significantly by subregion)
- Large wildlife hazards: hippos and crocodiles near water (statistically among the most dangerous large animals to humans in the region), plus region-specific large predators
- Scorpions in arid/savanna zones
