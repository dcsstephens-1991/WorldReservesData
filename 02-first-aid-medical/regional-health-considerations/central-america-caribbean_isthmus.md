# Central American Isthmus — Health Considerations

## Climate-driven risks
- Tropical climate with a Caribbean-side hurricane season (roughly June–November); heavy rains bring landslide risk on the region's steep volcanic terrain.
- Active volcanism across much of the isthmus — ashfall and evacuation planning are relevant near several active volcanoes.

## Disease patterns
- **Dengue, Zika, chikungunya** — all transmitted by the same widespread *Aedes* mosquito; bite prevention is the shared defense.
- **Malaria** — present in some rural/border areas, generally lower intensity than tropical South America or Sub-Saharan Africa.
- **Chagas disease** — spread by the triatomine ("kissing bug"), associated with traditional adobe/thatch housing where the insect can nest in wall cracks; more relevant in rural housing than urban areas.

## Dangerous species
See `06-animals-insects/` for identification detail:
- Fer-de-lance — responsible for a large share of serious snakebites in the region, found in both forest and agricultural land, sometimes near human settlement
- Eyelash viper, coral snake species
- Africanized honeybees present — more defensive than European honeybees, treat any hive disturbance as higher risk

## Notes
- Volcanic and seismic hazard planning is relevant across much of this subregion — cross-reference `01-quick-reference/emergency-type-triage.md`.
