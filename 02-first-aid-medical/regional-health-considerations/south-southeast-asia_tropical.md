# South & Southeast Asia (Tropical) — Health Considerations

## Climate-driven risks
- Monsoon-season flooding across much of the region — sharp seasonal spike in waterborne illness and vector-breeding.
- High heat/humidity year-round in lowland areas — heat exhaustion/heat stroke risk, especially for anyone not acclimatized.
- Cyclone/typhoon exposure along coastal areas (seasonal, timing varies by specific coast).

## Disease patterns
- **Dengue fever** (mosquito-borne, *Aedes* species, day-biting) — widespread across the region, no widely available field-level prophylaxis; mosquito bite prevention is the primary defense. Watch for high fever, severe headache, joint/muscle pain; severe dengue is a medical emergency (bleeding, plasma leakage).
- **Malaria** — present in many areas, especially rural/forested zones (lower risk in some dense urban areas) — bite prevention as above.
- **Japanese encephalitis** (mosquito-borne, rural rice-farming/pig-farming areas) — vaccine-preventable where a vaccine is reachable; otherwise bite prevention.
- **Typhoid, cholera, and other water/foodborne illness** — reinforces water purification and food safety discipline.
- **Leptospirosis** — from water contaminated with animal urine, especially after flooding; avoid wading through floodwater with open cuts.

## Dangerous species
See `06-animals-insects/` for identification detail. Named here for cross-reference:
- Venomous snakes: various cobra, krait, and viper species (specific species vary significantly by subregion — this needs subregion-level detail)
- Saltwater crocodiles in coastal/estuarine areas of parts of Southeast Asia
- Box jellyfish and other venomous marine life in some coastal waters
