# Sanitation & Hygiene

Poor sanitation is one of the largest drivers of preventable death in any prolonged crisis — more people die from resulting disease than from the initial event, historically. This is first aid at the population level.

## Priorities, in order
1. Safe water (see `03-water/` and `01-quick-reference/water-purification-quicksteps.md`)
2. Safe disposal of human waste
3. Handwashing
4. Food safety
5. Waste/vector (pest) control

## Human waste disposal
- **Never** near a water source — minimum ~30m (100ft) from any well, stream, or water storage, downhill/downstream of it.
- **Simple pit latrine**: dig a pit (at least 1-2m deep for a family, deeper for shared use), build a simple platform/seat over it, add a cover/lid to reduce flies and odor.
- Add a handful of ash or dry soil after each use — reduces odor, flies, and speeds decomposition.
- When a pit fills to within ~0.5m of the surface, cover it completely with soil and dig a new one.
- Group/shared settings: separate facilities are strongly preferred for privacy/dignity and uptake — people avoid using latrines they consider unsafe or exposed, which defeats the purpose.

## Handwashing
- The single most effective, lowest-cost disease prevention measure available.
- Critical times: after using the latrine, before preparing/eating food, after handling waste or a sick person, after handling animals.
- Soap and water is best; if no soap, ash or sand can serve as an abrasive substitute with water; alcohol-based hand sanitizer as a backup (less effective against some pathogens like norovirus).
- A simple "tippy tap" (a hanging container with a small hole, tipped by a foot pedal/lever) makes handwashing accessible without piped water.

## Food safety
- Cook food thoroughly, especially meat/eggs — heat kills most pathogens.
- Eat cooked food while hot; refrigeration may not be available, so avoid holding cooked food at room temperature for extended periods (bacterial growth risk).
- Keep raw meat separate from other food (surfaces, hands, containers) to avoid cross-contamination.
- Wash produce with treated water where possible.
- Discard food with mold (except a few specific safe cases like hard cheese — when in doubt, discard) or off smell/appearance.

## Waste and vector control
- Manage garbage — burn or bury non-recyclable waste away from living areas, to reduce rodent/insect attraction.
- Standing water breeds mosquitoes (malaria, dengue, and other disease vectors) — drain or cover stagnant water where possible.
- Basic pest control (netting over sleeping areas in mosquito-prone regions, keeping food sealed against rodents) meaningfully reduces disease transmission.

## Personal hygiene in constrained conditions
- Regular bathing, even with minimal water (sponge bathing), reduces skin infection and disease transmission.
- Change/wash clothing and bedding regularly where water allows — lice and other parasites spread through shared, unwashed fabric.
- Menstrual hygiene: clean, reusable cloth pads (washed with soap and dried fully, ideally in sunlight) are a workable low-resource option; access to private washing facilities matters for uptake and dignity.

## Managing illness within a group to limit spread
- Isolate people with diarrhea, vomiting, or respiratory symptoms from food preparation duties and, where possible, from close group contact until recovered.
- Designate a specific person/area for care of the sick to limit exposure across the group.
- Extra attention to handwashing and surface cleaning around anyone who is ill.
