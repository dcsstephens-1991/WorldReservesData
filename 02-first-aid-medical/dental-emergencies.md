# Dental Emergencies

Source: Hesperian *Where There Is No Dentist*-style field guidance. Dental pain/infection can become life-threatening (airway-obstructing swelling, spreading infection) if ignored — treat seriously.

## Toothache
- Rinse mouth with warm salt water (½ tsp salt per cup).
- Gently floss around the tooth to remove trapped food that may be causing pressure.
- Clove oil applied to the tooth/gum with a cotton swab is a traditional, reasonably effective topical analgesic if available.
- Avoid extreme hot/cold on the affected side.
- Persistent pain, swelling, or fever suggests infection/abscess — needs antibiotics if available and a dentist when reachable; don't ignore it.

## Dental abscess (swelling, throbbing pain, possible fever)
- This is a bacterial infection that can spread to the jaw, neck, and airway if untreated.
- Warm salt water rinses several times a day.
- Antibiotics if available and no allergy (a broad-spectrum option like amoxicillin is commonly used for dental infection where accessible).
- **Urgent evacuation signs**: swelling spreading to the eye or neck, difficulty swallowing or breathing, fever with facial swelling — this can become airway-threatening (Ludwig's angina) and is a true emergency.

## Knocked-out tooth (permanent tooth)
1. Pick it up by the crown (chewing surface), not the root.
2. If dirty, rinse briefly with clean water/milk — don't scrub or remove attached tissue fragments.
3. **Best outcome**: reinsert into the socket immediately if possible, have the person bite gently on clean gauze to hold it in place, seek a dentist within an hour if at all reachable.
4. If reinsertion isn't possible: store the tooth in milk, or the person's own saliva (inside the cheek), not dry and not in plain water — the root needs a compatible fluid to survive.
5. Time matters — viability drops fast after ~60 minutes out of the socket.
6. Baby (primary) teeth are generally not reinserted — consult a dentist if reachable, but this is lower urgency.

## Broken/chipped tooth
- Rinse mouth with warm water, save any broken fragment (store in milk).
- Cover a sharp edge with dental wax, sugar-free gum, or even soft candle wax to protect the tongue/cheek.
- See a dentist when reachable — even a "minor" chip can expose the nerve.

## Lost filling/crown
- Keep the crown if intact — can sometimes be temporarily re-cemented with dental cement (denture adhesive or even a small amount of toothpaste in a pinch, temporary only) until proper care is reachable.
- Avoid chewing on that side.

## Object caught between teeth
- Floss gently to remove — do not use sharp metal objects that could cut the gum or damage enamel.

## Basic prevention (important when dental care access is limited)
- Brush twice daily even with improvised tools (a frayed twig "chew stick" from a non-toxic tree species works in a pinch, or salt on a cloth-wrapped finger).
- Limit sugar/sticky foods, especially without regular brushing access.
- Rinse with water after meals if brushing isn't possible.
