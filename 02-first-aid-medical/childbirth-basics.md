# Childbirth Basics (Emergency, Unattended)

For situations with no trained birth attendant available. This is not a substitute for prenatal care or a trained midwife/doctor when any is reachable — seek one if at all possible before labor.

## Before labor — if you have any advance notice
- Identify the nearest source of skilled help (clinic, midwife, hospital) and a way to reach it.
- Prepare clean supplies: clean cloths/towels, a clean blanket for the baby, something to tie the cord (clean string/shoelace, boiled first if possible), clean scissors or blade (boiled/flamed to sterilize), a container for the placenta.
- Clean water and soap for handwashing.

## Recognizing labor
- Regular, increasingly strong/frequent contractions (tightening across the belly).
- "Water breaking" — fluid gush or trickle.
- Bloody show (mucus with streaks of blood).
- First-time births: often many hours from first signs to delivery — there is usually more time than it feels like. Repeat births tend to progress faster.

## During labor
- Let the person move/position however is most comfortable — squatting, hands and knees, side-lying, or standing are all normal; there's no single required position.
- Encourage slow breathing through contractions, rest between them.
- Do not attempt to speed things up by pulling, pushing on the belly, or internal exams unless trained.

## Delivery — what to do
1. Wash your hands thoroughly.
2. As the head crowns (becomes visible), support it gently — do not pull.
3. Once the head is out, check gently if the cord is looped around the neck — if so and it's loose, slip it over the head; if tight, do not panic, most births still proceed fine, keep going.
4. The body usually follows within one more contraction/push. Support the baby as it emerges — newborns are slippery.
5. **Immediately dry and warm the baby** — this is one of the most important steps. Place on the parent's bare chest, cover both with a blanket.
6. The baby should begin crying/breathing within the first minute. If not: rub the back firmly, flick the soles of the feet, clear the mouth/nose gently with a clean cloth. If still not breathing after this, begin infant rescue breaths (small, gentle puffs, ~40-60/min) — this is different from adult CPR technique, use much less force.

## After delivery
- **Cord**: no rush to cut it — it can be left attached for several minutes to hours; a placenta following in a bucket/bag is manageable. If cutting: tie tightly in two places a few inches apart, cut between the ties with a sterile blade.
- **Placenta**: usually delivers within 5-30 min after the baby, with mild contractions. Do not pull on the cord to force it. Save it if a provider can examine it later (should be delivered whole); heavy ongoing bleeding after delivery without the placenta coming is an emergency — evacuate urgently.
- **Bleeding after birth**: some bleeding is normal. Uterine massage (firmly rubbing the lower belly in circular motions) after the placenta delivers helps the uterus contract and reduces bleeding. Soaking more than one large pad per hour, or persistent heavy bleeding, is an emergency.
- **Breastfeeding**: putting the baby to the breast soon after birth helps the uterus contract (reduces bleeding) and starts feeding — even before milk fully comes in, colostrum (early milk) is valuable.

## Warning signs requiring urgent evacuation, if any resource is reachable
- Heavy bleeding (soaking through cloths rapidly) before or after delivery
- Baby not delivered after many hours of strong, regular pushing (obstructed labor)
- Cord or a limb presenting first instead of the head (malposition)
- Seizures, severe headache, or vision changes in the parent (possible eclampsia)
- Baby not breathing after rescue breath attempts
- High fever in parent or baby in the days following birth

## Cultural/religious note
Birth practices, who is permitted in the room, placenta handling, and naming/ritual timing vary widely by tradition — see `religious-cultural-considerations.md`. Defer to the family's wishes wherever it doesn't conflict with an immediate safety step above.
