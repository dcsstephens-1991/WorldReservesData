# Off-Grid Electrical Basics

## Solar power fundamentals
- **Panel → charge controller → battery → inverter (if AC needed)** is the standard basic system chain. The charge controller prevents overcharging the battery; the inverter converts stored DC power to AC for standard household devices, if needed (many devices can also run directly on DC, avoiding inverter losses).
- Panel output is rated in watts under ideal conditions — real-world output varies with sun angle, cloud cover, panel temperature, and dust/dirt accumulation; plan for meaningfully less than the rated output on average.
- System sizing starts from your actual power needs (add up device wattage × hours used per day) rather than from an arbitrary panel size — oversizing wastes money, undersizing leaves you short.

## Basic wiring safety
- Match wire gauge to the current (amperage) it will carry — undersized wire overheats and is a fire risk; this matters more than most people expect, especially over longer wire runs.
- Fuses/breakers sized appropriately for the circuit protect against overcurrent — don't bypass or oversize a fuse "to stop it tripping," that removes the safety margin it exists for.
- Batteries (especially lead-acid) can produce explosive hydrogen gas while charging — ventilate battery storage/charging areas, keep open flame and sparks away.
- Always treat any circuit as live until confirmed otherwise with a meter, not by assumption.

## Small-scale wind power
- Effective mainly in consistently windy sites — average wind speed matters far more than peak gusts for realistic power output.
- Requires a sturdy tower/mount given the forces involved in wind — a poorly mounted turbine is both an underperformer and a hazard.

## Basic system components worth understanding
- **Charge controller types**: PWM (simpler, cheaper) vs. MPPT (more efficient, especially with larger arrays or cooler climates) — MPPT is generally worth the added cost for anything beyond a small system.
- **Battery types**: lead-acid (cheaper, shorter lifespan, needs ventilation) vs. lithium (more expensive, longer lifespan, more forgiving of partial discharge) — either can work for an off-grid system, the choice depends on budget and maintenance tolerance.
