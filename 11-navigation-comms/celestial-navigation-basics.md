# Celestial Navigation Basics

Finding direction and, with more skill, approximate position, using the sun, stars, and moon — useful when a compass isn't available or to cross-check one.

## Finding direction from the sun
- The sun rises roughly in the east and sets roughly in the west, though the exact point shifts seasonally (further north/south of due east-west depending on season and latitude) — a rough but genuinely useful default in the absence of anything more precise.
- **Shadow-stick method**: place a stick vertically in the ground, mark the tip of its shadow, wait 15-20 minutes, mark the new shadow tip position — the line between the two marks runs roughly east-west (first mark toward west, second toward east, in the Northern Hemisphere), letting you derive north-south from a line perpendicular to it. This is a well-established, low-tech, reasonably reliable method.
- **Analog watch method** (Northern Hemisphere): point the hour hand at the sun, the line halfway between the hour hand and 12 o'clock points roughly south (adjust for daylight saving time if applicable). This method is less precise than the shadow-stick method but requires no waiting.

## Finding direction from the stars
- **Northern Hemisphere**: Polaris (the North Star) sits very close to true north and doesn't appear to move as the sky rotates around it, making it a reliable fixed reference — find it via the two "pointer stars" at the end of the Big Dipper's bowl, which point roughly toward Polaris.
- **Southern Hemisphere**: there is no equally bright star at the southern celestial pole; the Southern Cross constellation is commonly used instead — a line extended from the long axis of the cross, combined with a line from two bright "pointer stars" nearby, roughly indicates the direction of the south celestial pole where they cross.

## General principles
- Celestial navigation is more reliable for direction-finding than for precise position-finding without additional tools (a sextant and detailed almanac/calculation, which is a much deeper skill) — treat the methods above as direction aids, not precise positioning tools.
- Combine with dead reckoning (`map-compass-basics.md`) for more reliable travel over distance than either method alone.
