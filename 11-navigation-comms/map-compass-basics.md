# Map & Compass Basics

## Reading a topographic map
- Contour lines represent elevation — closely spaced lines mean steep terrain, widely spaced lines mean gentle terrain; learning to read the shape of the land from contour patterns (valleys, ridges, peaks, saddles) is the core map-reading skill.
- Scale tells you the real-world distance represented by a given map distance — always check the scale before estimating travel distance/time.
- Grid systems (like UTM or a national grid) allow precise position reporting as coordinates — worth understanding at least one grid system, in addition to latitude/longitude, since some grid systems are easier for quick manual position-plotting in the field.

## Using a compass
- A compass points to magnetic north, which differs from true (geographic) north by an amount called **declination**, which varies by location and changes slowly over time — for accurate navigation, adjust for the declination specific to your area (marked on most quality topographic maps for their date of printing) rather than assuming magnetic and true north are the same.
- Basic technique: orient the map so its features align with the compass (accounting for declination), identify recognizable landmarks matching map and terrain, and take a bearing (a compass direction) toward your destination or a landmark to travel by.
- Keep a compass away from metal objects/electronics that can deflect the needle and give a false reading.

## Dead reckoning (navigating without landmarks)
- Track direction of travel (compass bearing) and distance (pace count — the number of steps to cover a known distance, calibrated to your own stride) to estimate position when landmarks aren't visible (dense forest, fog, night travel).
- Error accumulates over distance with this method — cross-check against any available landmark or feature periodically to correct drift.

## Latitude and longitude basics
- Latitude measures position north/south of the equator (0° at the equator, 90° at the poles); longitude measures position east/west of the prime meridian.
- This is the same coordinate system used throughout this archive's regional taxonomy (`docs/REGIONS.md`) — understanding it is directly useful both for navigation and for locating yourself within this archive's regional content.
