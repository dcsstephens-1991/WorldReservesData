# Livestock Basics

Starter overview for small-scale animal keeping. Cross-reference `04-food-agriculture/` for feed/food-system integration.

## Chickens
- Low barrier to entry, fast return (eggs within ~5-6 months from chick, meat birds faster), omnivorous (kitchen scraps, bugs, feed).
- Basic needs: predator-proof coop (secure at night especially), ~2-4 sq ft per bird indoor space, nesting boxes, clean water, roosting bars.
- Common issues: mites/lice, respiratory illness in damp/poorly ventilated coops, predation (foxes, raccoons, hawks, snakes taking eggs).

## Goats
- Good for milk and meat in varied terrain, including marginal land unsuitable for crops; browse rather than graze (eat brush/shrubs preferentially).
- Basic needs: secure fencing (goats are notorious escape artists — climb and push through weak points), shelter from rain/wind, mineral supplementation, hoof trimming.
- Common issues: internal parasites (rotate pasture where possible), bloat if given excess rich feed too quickly.

## Rabbits
- Fast breeding cycle, efficient feed-to-meat conversion, quiet, low land requirement relative to larger livestock.
- Basic needs: secure hutch/cage (protected from predators and temperature extremes), consistent feed (hay-heavy diet), clean water.
- Common issues: heat stress (rabbits are vulnerable to high heat), respiratory illness in damp conditions.

## General livestock principles
- Water access and quality matters as much as feed — check daily.
- Isolate sick animals promptly to limit spread within a small herd/flock.
- Breeding pair/small-group planning matters for sustainability — avoid inbreeding depression by planning genetic diversity (multiple unrelated breeding lines) from the start if building a long-term herd.
- Vaccination/veterinary access will likely not exist in a prolonged crisis — basic husbandry (clean water, adequate space, good nutrition, prompt isolation of sick animals) is the primary disease-prevention tool available.
