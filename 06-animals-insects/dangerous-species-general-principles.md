# Dangerous Species — General Principles

General, non-regional principles for assessing animal/insect danger. Pair with `regional/` (once populated) for species-specific identification, and `02-first-aid-medical/trauma-and-wound-care.md` for bite/sting treatment.

## General venomous-snake recognition (not universal — always defer to regional guides)
- No single reliable global visual rule exists ("triangular head," "vertical pupils," etc. all have exceptions by region) — the only fully reliable method is knowing the specific species present in your area.
- Default behavior around any unidentified snake: leave it alone, keep a wide distance, do not attempt to handle or kill it — most bites happen when someone tries to interact with the animal.
- Watch step placement and hand placement (rocks, logs, gathering firewood) in any region with venomous species — most bites are defensive, on the hands/feet, from an animal that was startled or grabbed blind.

## General principles for large/predatory animals
- Most large predators avoid humans by default; encounters are more common when animals are habituated to human food (secure food/garbage, never feed wildlife) or surprised at close range.
- Making noise while moving through dense/limited-visibility terrain reduces surprise encounters.
- Group size deters most predators — solo travel in known predator habitat carries higher risk.
- Know the specific "what to do" for locally present large predators (e.g. play dead vs. fight back varies by species — bears are not uniform on this) — see regional files as they're built.

## Insects and disease vectors
- Most serious insect-related harm globally comes from disease transmission (mosquitoes, ticks) rather than direct venom — see `02-first-aid-medical/regional-health-considerations/` for regional disease patterns tied to specific vectors.
- Basic protective measures apply broadly: covering skin at dawn/dusk (peak mosquito activity for many species), permethrin-treated clothing, checking for ticks after time in brush, sealing food against rodents/insects.

## General sting/bite response
- See `02-first-aid-medical/trauma-and-wound-care.md` ("Animal/insect bites and stings") for immediate response steps.
- Note distinguishing features of the animal if safely possible, without risking further exposure — helps identify appropriate antivenom/treatment.
