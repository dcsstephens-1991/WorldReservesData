# Edible Insects — Basics

Entomophagy (eating insects) is a well-established, high-protein food source in much of the world, though culturally unfamiliar in others. Basic safety and starting points.

## General safety principles
- **Always cook insects before eating** — reduces parasite/pathogen risk significantly; raw insects carry meaningfully higher risk than raw plants.
- **Avoid brightly colored insects** — bright coloration is often (not always, but commonly enough to be a useful default) a warning signal (aposematism) for toxicity in the insect world.
- **Avoid insects with a strong odor when disturbed** — often indicates a chemical defense compound.
- **Avoid any insect that has been in contact with pesticides** — insects from treated agricultural areas or gardens may carry chemical residue.
- People with shellfish allergies should be cautious — insects share some of the same proteins (chitin, tropomyosin) that trigger shellfish allergic reactions.

## Commonly eaten, widely corroborated categories
- **Crickets and grasshoppers** — widely eaten globally, generally considered safe when cooked, avoid ones that may have contacted pesticides.
- **Mealworms** (beetle larvae) — easy to farm in a controlled setting (see below), commonly eaten, low risk since farmed rather than wild-caught.
- **Termites and ants** — eaten in many cultures, generally safe cooked; avoid ants with strong chemical defenses (some large ant species have painful/toxic bites, not just stings).

## What to avoid entirely
- Any insect you cannot identify with confidence.
- Caterpillars/larvae in general, unless specifically known-safe for your region — many have irritant hairs or sequester plant toxins from what they eat.
- Spiders, scorpions, and other arachnids are a separate category from insects and carry higher identification/safety risk — not covered by general insect-eating guidance.

## Small-scale insect farming (lower risk than wild foraging)
- **Mealworms** — easy to raise in a container with bran/oats as substrate and occasional vegetable scraps for moisture; a well-documented, low-effort protein source.
- **Black soldier fly larvae** — used in some small-scale systems to process food waste into a protein source; requires more specific setup knowledge.
- Farming known, controlled insect populations avoids the wild-identification risk entirely, similar to the mushroom-cultivation-over-foraging principle in `05-plants-fungi/fungi-foraging-safety.md`.
