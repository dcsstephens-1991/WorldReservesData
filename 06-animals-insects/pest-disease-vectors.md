# Pest & Disease Vector Management

Cross-references `02-first-aid-medical/sanitation-hygiene.md` and the regional disease patterns in `02-first-aid-medical/regional-health-considerations/`.

## Mosquitoes
- Eliminate standing water near living areas — even small amounts (bottle caps, tire ruts, blocked gutters) are enough to breed.
- Netting (ideally insecticide-treated) over sleeping areas in mosquito-prone regions is one of the highest-value, lowest-cost interventions available for malaria/dengue/other mosquito-borne disease prevention.
- Cover skin and use repellent at dawn/dusk in regions with day-and-dusk-biting species.

## Ticks
- Check thoroughly after time in brush/tall grass/wooded areas — a tick generally needs many hours of attachment to transmit disease, so prompt removal meaningfully reduces risk.
- Remove with fine-tipped tweezers, grasping close to the skin, pulling straight out with steady pressure — don't twist, don't use folk methods (heat, petroleum jelly) which can increase disease transmission risk by agitating the tick.
- Light-colored clothing makes ticks easier to spot before they attach.

## Rodents
- Seal food storage completely — rodents both eat stored food and contaminate remaining food with urine/droppings (a disease transmission route, see hantavirus/Lassa fever notes in relevant regional health files).
- Keep living/storage areas clear of clutter that provides nesting habitat.
- Ventilate before cleaning any area with heavy rodent droppings, avoid dry-sweeping (aerosolizes contaminated dust) — dampen first.

## Flies and general sanitation pests
- Directly tied to waste management — see `02-first-aid-medical/sanitation-hygiene.md` for latrine covering/ash application and garbage management, both of which are the primary fly-control method available without pesticides.

## Stored-food pests (weevils, grain moths, etc.)
- Store dry goods in sealed, hard containers (glass, metal, thick plastic) rather than original packaging.
- Diatomaceous earth (food-grade) mixed into stored grain is a widely used, low-tech deterrent for some stored-product pests.
- Inspect stored food periodically; discard visibly infested stock rather than risking spread to clean stores.
