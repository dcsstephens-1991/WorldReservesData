# Temperate North America — Regional Plant Notes

## Category overview
- Most of `common-survival-plants_global.md` (cattail, dandelion, plantain, stinging nettle, chickweed, oak acorns) are present and common across this entire subregion.
- The carrot/parsley family caution in `toxic-plants-widely-confused.md` is directly relevant here — water hemlock and poison hemlock are both present across most of temperate North America, especially near water and disturbed ground respectively.
- Poison ivy, poison oak, and poison sumac (*Toxicodendron* species) are a distinct, extremely common regional hazard — not toxic to eat (rarely eaten at all) but cause a severe allergic skin reaction (urushiol) on contact for most people. Widely known identifying rule of thumb: "leaves of three, let it be" for poison ivy/oak (compound leaves in groups of three leaflets) — a genuinely useful and widely corroborated field heuristic for this specific plant, unlike most such rhymes.

## Species entries

### Poison ivy / poison oak (*Toxicodendron radicans* and relatives)
- **Identifying features**: compound leaves in groups of three leaflets, leaflet edges can be smooth, toothed, or lobed (variable — don't rely on edge shape alone), often reddish in new spring growth and again in fall, can grow as a low plant, climbing vine, or shrub depending on region and conditions.
- **Hazard**: urushiol oil on all parts of the plant (including in smoke if burned — inhaling burning poison ivy is a serious respiratory hazard, never burn it) causes an itchy, blistering rash on contact for most people, developing over 12-72 hours.
- **Response to contact**: wash exposed skin thoroughly with soap and water as soon as possible (reduces but doesn't eliminate reaction risk), wash any clothing/tools that contacted it (oil stays active on surfaces for a long time), calamine lotion/antihistamines for symptom relief once a reaction develops, seek care for a severe or widespread reaction, facial involvement, or difficulty breathing (possible smoke inhalation history).
- **Source**: widely corroborated across North American extension office and outdoor safety guidance.
